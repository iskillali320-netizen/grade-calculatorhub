College & High School GPA Calculator

Our GPA Calculator is designed to help students quickly and accurately determine their Grade Point Average on a standard 4.0 scale. Whether you're tracking your semester progress in college or calculating your cumulative high school GPA for college applications, this tool makes the math effortless by factoring in both your letter grades and the credit hours for each course.

How to Use This Calculator

1
Enter the Course Name (Optional): Keep your calculations organized by typing in the name of the class, like "Biology" or "History 101".
2
Select Your Letter Grade: Choose the grade you received (or expect to receive) from the dropdown menu. This will automatically convert your letter grade to a 4.0 scale point value.
3
Enter the Credits: Input the number of credit hours the course is worth. If your high school uses a system where every class is 1 credit, just enter "1" for all courses.

Example Calculation

Let's say you took three college courses this semester with varying credit hours:

Calculus (4 credits): Grade B+ (3.3 points)

English (3 credits): Grade A (4.0 points)

Physical Ed (1 credit): Grade A- (3.7 points)

Result: Your calculated GPA is 3.61.

(Math: [ (4×3.3) + (3×4.0) + (1×3.7) ] / 8 total credits)

The Standard 4.0 GPA Formula

To calculate your GPA by hand, you need to find your total "Grade Points" and divide by your total "Credits."

GPA = (Total Grade Points Earned) / (Total Credit Hours Attempted)

Note: "Grade Points Earned" for a single class is found by multiplying the course's credit hours by the grade value on the 4.0 scale.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good GPA?

In the United States, a 3.0 GPA (a B average) is generally considered "good" and is the standard requirement for many scholarships and programs. Top-tier universities often look for GPAs closer to a 3.8 or 4.0.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

An unweighted GPA measures your grades on a flat 4.0 scale, regardless of class difficulty. A weighted GPA (often used in high schools) gives extra points for Honors or AP classes, usually on a 5.0 scale (where an A is worth 5.0 instead of 4.0).

Does this calculator work for both college and high school?

Yes! For college, simply enter your course credits (e.g., 3 or 4). For high school, if your school doesn't use varying credits, simply enter "1" for the credit value of every class.

What if I retake a class?

School policies vary. Many colleges offer "grade forgiveness," where the new grade replaces the old one in your GPA calculation. Check your student handbook to be sure!
